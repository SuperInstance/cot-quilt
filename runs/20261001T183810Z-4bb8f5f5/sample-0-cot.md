## answer

Assume the cellular graph is finite and connected, and let the “next unit” be carried by a token. The cell holding the token is the cell that receives the next unit.

### Minimal local rule

At cell \(i\), with potential \(p_i\) and resistance \(r_i\):

1. For each \(j \in \{i\} \cup N(i)\), compute a local weight
   \[
   w_j = \varepsilon + \frac{p_j}{r_j+1},
   \]
   where \(\varepsilon > 0\) is a small constant.
2. Choose \(j\) with probability proportional to \(w_j\):
   \[
   \Pr(j)=\frac{w_j}{\sum_{k\in\{i\}\cup N(i)} w_k}.
   \]
3. If \(j=i\), \(i\) keeps the token and receives the next unit.  
   If \(j\neq i\), \(i\) passes the token to \(j\), and \(j\) receives the next unit.

This uses only \(p_j,r_j\) for the current cell and its direct neighbors. No global view is needed.

### Why it is fair and cannot starve a cell forever

Because \(\varepsilon>0\), every neighbor and the current cell have a positive probability of being chosen at each step. The token’s position therefore evolves as a Markov chain on the finite graph.

- Since the graph is connected and every allowed transition has positive probability, the chain is **irreducible**.
- Since the current cell can choose itself, there is a positive self-loop probability, so the chain is **aperiodic**.
- A finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain visits every state infinitely often with probability 1.

So every cell receives the token—and therefore budget—infinitely often. No cell can be starved forever.

The potential/resistance values bias which cells are chosen more often, but the \(\varepsilon\)-term guarantees that no cell is ever excluded permanently.

If randomness is forbidden, then the stated static potentials/resistances alone are not enough to guarantee no starvation: a cell with very low potential and very high resistance could be permanently ignored by any purely preference-based local rule. In that case one must add an explicit local aging or round-robin mechanism. The rule above is the minimal stochastic local way to combine preference with a no-starvation guarantee.
